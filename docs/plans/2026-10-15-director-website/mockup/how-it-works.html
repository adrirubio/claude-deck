How it works: one piece of work, step by step

This page follows one example from a GitHub issue to a merged pull request (PR): “Add a short help note to the welcome page.” It is an example, not a measured delivery. It shows the roles, the screens, and the gates.

1

The Director writes the issue

The Director writes the issue with a clear result, orders the backlog, and marks the issue ready.

What you see: GitHub shows the issue and its place in the backlog. Deck does not replace GitHub.

Your decision: the goal, the order, and the ready mark.

2

The owner proposes a plan. The Leader approves it.

One owner is assigned. The owner proposes a short plan. The designated Leader approves it, and only then does building start.

What you see: Overview shows progress and what needs you. Work shows the owner and the waiting reason. Teams shows the roster and the named Leader.

Your decision: the outcome and the limits. The Leader approves agent plans inside them.

3

A member does the work

The member builds the change in a separate working copy for this job, runs the scoped checks, and opens a PR — one proposed change.

What you see: Work shows the owner, the state, and the PR link. Repositories shows which backlog feeds the team.

Your decision: none here, unless the team needs a decision only you can make, or a safety stop applies.

4

Reviewers check the work

An independent reviewer reads the exact version proposed. Findings go back to the owner, who fixes them on the same PR. Automatic checks (CI) run again on the new version.

What you see: Work shows whether the item waits for review. Audit shows delivery measures with their limits.

Your decision: review follows your repository rules. Passing checks alone give nobody the power to merge.

5

The Supervisor watches and helps — when you assign one

The Supervisor is optional. When assigned, it fixes routine blockers inside the authority you gave it. When only you can decide, it explains what you need to decide and gives you the evidence.

What you see: Overview shows whether the Supervisor is healthy, late, or absent.

Your decision: grant or withdraw that authority, and keep publication, budgets, models, and safety stops.

6

The PR merges, and the record stays

The PR merges under your repository rules, after review and checks pass. The issue closes, and the audit record keeps what happened.

What you see: Work shows the item finished. Audit shows the delivery history with its limits. Some audit detail sits behind an access gate.

Your decision: accept the result and decide publication.

Each screen above is a real Deck screen, shown with seeded example data in the plan. Seeded data is not live work, and this example is not a measured delivery time.

Words for this page

Harness
The agent tool a member runs, such as Claude Code or Codex.
Scope
The link that connects one team to one repository and its dispatch labels.
Work item
One tracked piece of work from issue to review.
Approval
A recorded yes at a gate, such as plan approval or PR review.
Agent Mail
The team mailbox where agents coordinate and hand off work.
Supervisor
The outside watcher that repairs routine faults under delegation.
Audit
The ledger that records what the factory did and its limits.
CI
Automatic checks that run on a PR before merge.
PR
A pull request is a proposed change reviewed under repository policy.

What this page does not promise

No time saving is measured yet, and the full cost of delivering a feature is not known. Deck can show estimates for Claude Code sessions when their data is available. A person still writes the goal, sets the limits, and decides publication. Deck runs on your own computer and does not replace GitHub.
